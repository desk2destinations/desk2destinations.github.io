Hiroshima: A Quiet Reckoning & a City That Rebuilt Itself

We caught the 7:50am Shinkansen from Osaka to Hiroshima. Even on our third Shinkansen ride, the comfort and design still surprised us — wide seats, almost no sound, snack trolleys gliding through like clockwork. Under ninety minutes later we stepped off the train into a city with a very different weight to it.

We dropped our bags and walked straight to the A-Bomb Dome. There is no easy way to describe standing in front of it. The skeleton of the building has been left exactly as it was on the morning of August 6, 1945 — every brick a deliberate refusal to forget. We crossed into Peace Memorial Park and spent the rest of the morning at the museum. There is a 3D projection sequence that reconstructs the bombing, and it stays with you. We didn't speak much when we came out.

For lunch we'd hoped to try Hiroshima's signature okonomiyaki, but the most-recommended spot had a queue going around the block and we had a bus to catch. We ended up at a small Indian restaurant nearby — sometimes the comfort of familiar food after a heavy morning is exactly the right call.

After the Museum: Hondori, a Lakeside Sorbet & the Loop Bus

In the afternoon, we let the city do the lifting. We wandered through Hondori, did a little shopping (Sketchers found their way into a bag — Hiroshima retail prices are friendlier than central Tokyo), and ended up at a lake near the park, eating lemon sorbet while ducks drifted past. We took the Hiroshima sightseeing loop bus — the red one — through a few more neighbourhoods to feel out the regular city: tram lines, riverside walks, schoolkids cycling home.

If you only saw the rest of Hiroshima — the trams, the kids on bikes, the lemon sorbet by the lake — you would never believe what this city has lived through. That, in itself, is the lesson.

The Overnight Bus Back to Tokyo

We had to skip Miyajima. The round trip to the floating torii alone is around 2.5 hours, and our overnight Willer bus to Tokyo was at 6:05pm. It's the one regret of the day, and the reason we'll come back. The bus, as it happened, pulled out at 6:02 — three minutes early, very on-brand for Japan. The highway rest stops were spotless, the seats were comfortable enough to actually sleep on, and we woke up rolling into Tokyo.

Must-Do in Hiroshima

  • A-Bomb Dome: Stand in front of it. Read the plaque. Take your time.
  • Peace Memorial Museum: The 3D projection is essential and difficult.
  • Peace Memorial Park: Walk it slowly — every monument has a story.
  • Hiroshima Okonomiyaki: Layered with noodles. Worth queueing for.
  • Miyajima Island: Plan a half-day — don't squeeze it like we did.
  • Hondori Arcade: Covered shopping street near the park.
  • Hiroshima Sightseeing Loop Bus: Easy way to see the regular city.
  • Lakeside Lemon Sorbet: After the museum, you'll need it.
