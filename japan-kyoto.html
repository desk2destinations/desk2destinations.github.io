Kyoto: Matcha, Temples, Rain & Our Fifth Anniversary Together

We rolled into Kyoto at 5:30am with the morning still chilly around us. The metro carried us to Kiori Exec by 6:30am — far too early for check-in, but the staff promised to get us a room as soon as one was ready. From here on, no more shared rooms, no more shared washrooms. The hostel chapter of the trip was over. The plan for the day was Uji, the famous matcha town, but nothing opened before 9:30am, and the temples — beautiful as they would have been — didn't have our enthusiasm yet. We'd just done Senso-ji in Tokyo and our temple cup was full. I sketched out a Uji map, then promptly fell asleep in the waiting area.

Our room came through at 10am. We were sleepy enough to crash again, then finally got ready and reached Uji at 4:30pm. Yes, late — Uji is nearly an hour from Kyoto Station. We'd starred countless matcha shops on the map but only got to two; most close by 5:30 or 6pm. Still, that was enough to fall in. We tried our first proper matcha parfait, matcha tea, matcha candies, and stocked up for home — tea, latte, candies, even matcha noodles. Matcha is one of those things that grows on you with every sip, every spoonful.

With the shops shut, we wandered — the lake, Uji Shrine, a quiet supermarket, the famous statues from the Tales of Genji. Uji at sunset is its own little reward. By 8pm we were back in Kyoto, and at Vegan Izakaya we were told they were fully booked — until a small request earned us a table. Their gyoza turned out to be the best we'd eat in Japan. We tried sake, the legs gave up, and we still walked back to the hotel anyway. Before sleep, a small bit of admin: Suica was costing us 320 yen a ride, so for tomorrow's big day I'd switch us to a day bus pass — cheaper at four rides or more. Map sorted. Lights out.

Five years… half a decade with Golu. It feels new, it still feels fresh, and it still is a very happy place. He makes it. No matter how the world perceives me, I know he has seen the worst in me and still chooses to stay. That is where I know I am lucky and grateful.

Day 1 (23 Apr): Our Anniversary

Our anniversary. Five years. I genuinely cannot believe half a decade has passed with Golu. It feels new, it still feels fresh, and it still is a very happy place. He makes it. No matter how the world perceives me, I know he has seen the worst in me and he still chooses to stay. That's where I know I am lucky — that the universe lets him belong to me. People say you get used to each other, that the spark fades — but no, with him, he still irritates me, I'm still not habitual of his discipline and his cleanliness 😛. He is literally the ground to my live craziness. We've been to many countries and hopefully life will be kind enough to keep this going. We've had our highs and our blunders along the way, but as long as we're together, I have the energy to make any of it right.

We got into our new clothes, picked up our transport tickets. The weather did its breezy Kyoto thing and then started drizzling exactly when we stepped out. Two umbrellas borrowed from the hotel saved the entire day — it rained hard.

First stop was the famous Kiyomizudera and the market that fans out below it. The temples and shrines in Japan are awe-inspiring in a quieter, more orderly way than the ones we grew up around in India. After the temple, we walked the two iconic streets, Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, tried local sweets, ate apple pie. The rain pushed us into a Starbucks — and even Starbucks is different here, with Japanese-style floor seating. We sat for a while, drank coffee, planned the next move. Kinkakuji, the Golden Temple, won the vote.

Once inside Kinkakuji I realised I'd lost my day pass. You can't go back once you're in, but a guard was kind enough to check anyway. Nothing turned up. I felt awful. And then Harshit's positivity stepped in — three bus rides already, fine, we'd be fine. Even now, writing this, it still nags at me a little; but I think it's okay as long as we're together 😛.

The covered markets in Kyoto are an underrated travel hack. Rain doesn't touch you; you can stroll, try things, drift. We shopped souvenirs, did a full sweep of 3 Coins — hairbrush, foldable chair, the works. Then a bookstore inside a mall, two whole floors of books. The reading culture in Japan is something else, the love for their own language even more so: across two floors, one shelf carried English translations. Every time I walk into a bookstore I feel quietly small — there's just so much left to read.

Rain ruled out the Fushimi Inari plan. We circled back to the market, and that's when I spotted an Indian restaurant. On a rainy day, your heart wants comfort food. Delhi Restaurant in Kyoto — chole bhature, onion parantha, run by a Dehradun-wala for 20 years. On breezy days like this you walk on the warmth of the love your partner gives you. I would not have planned this anniversary any differently than to see the satisfaction on Harshit's face after a plate of Indian food. I have a thousand things racing through my mind; he's calm and sweet, and even on crazy journeys his comfort still lives in Indian food at home. So I feel quietly lucky to come from a populated ethnicity — his comfort, I can find for him almost anywhere in the world 😀.

Day 2 (24 Apr): The Fushimi Inari Day

The famous Fushimi Inari day, and our last in Kyoto. We've been slow-paced this whole trip and today's wake-up was no exception: we started around noon. Brunch was at Ain Soph Kyoto — we'd already loved Ain Soph Ginza in Tokyo, and this one held the line. Then over to The Macha House for a matcha parfait. Wow. Simply wow. We added the seasonal matcha strawberry sakura latte and that, too, was outstanding.

Heavy brunch behind us, Golu was set on the Fushimi Inari hike. After much back-and-forth we decided not to do the traditional kimono — I'd originally planned it for our anniversary, but the rain killed that, and now we'd rather spend the money on another matcha parfait 😛. We reached Fushimi Inari around 1:15pm and started up. Walking through those endless Torii gates hits differently than anything you can describe — you don't have to read the kanji on each one for the place to get under your skin. We climbed to a viewpoint where the city opens up below, and Harshit got mildly annoyed by my speed on the way down. He was back to normal by the time we hit the bottom. More photos, more watching people pray and tie wishes.

The market around Fushimi Inari is mostly non-veg, so we headed back to central Kyoto for round two of shopping — this time skincare. After some research I'd locked in on OS Drugstore as the best price point, with Matsumoto Kiyoshi as a comparison stop. We bounced between the two for the best deals, plus gums, plus jellies — too many products, too many translations.

Dinner was the very famous Kyoto Engine. One absolute MUST EAT. We finished two bowls of ramen in no time. The best ramen we've ever had. I crave it just writing this. Who would have imagined that vegetarian chilli ramen would be the highlight of a Japanese trip? Naturally, a parfait followed for dessert — Kyoto Engine had what felt like a thousand varieties on display. With so much sugar in us, we walked back to the hotel to enjoy the last night. Then came the pack: Kyoto to Osaka, route figured out, two suitcases, one backpack, all the shopping.

Kyoto teaches you to slow down — not because you have to, but because the city refuses to be rushed. Even the rain in Kyoto walks.

Must-Do in Kyoto

  • Uji Town for Matcha: Go early, most shops close by 6pm.
  • Kiyomizudera & Sannenzaka: Best morning experience.
  • Kinkakuji (Golden Temple): The real thing, even in rain.
  • Fushimi Inari Hike: Walk as far as the viewpoint at least.
  • Vegan Izakaya: The gyoza — don't miss it.
  • Kyoto Engine Ramen: The best veg ramen we've ever had.
  • Covered Market Strolling: Rain or shine, a delight.
  • Day Bus Pass: Worth it if you plan 4+ bus rides.
