Osaka: Shoe Heaven, Osaka Castle & the Glow of Dotonbori

Day 1 (25 Apr): Rinku Outlet Shoe Spree & Rikuro's Cheesecake

We rolled into Osaka with a single mission for day one — shoes. The Rinku Premium Outlet sits right next to Kansai airport, so we dropped the heavier bags at the hotel and made straight for it. Rinku is sprawling, open-air, breezy, and packed with the kind of brands that would cost double back home. We started at Nike, moved through Sketchers, then ASICS, and finally landed at Onitsuka Tiger, where I think we tried on more pairs than the staff had seen all week. Four boxes later, we forced ourselves to stop.

By the time we crawled back into central Osaka, our feet were burning and our wallets were lighter. There was only one cure: Rikuro's at Daimaru. Watching them flip those wobbly jiggly cheesecakes is half the experience. We grabbed one to go and ate it sitting on a bench outside, still warm, almost soufflé-light. Dinner was a small ramen shop near the hotel — nothing fancy, exactly what tired legs needed. Asleep before 11.

Day 2 (26 Apr): Osaka Castle, Don Quijote & Dotonbori

Today was the proper sightseeing day. We started at Osaka Castle. The grounds are enormous, and the castle itself looks unreal — gold detailing catching the morning sun, surrounded by moats and stone walls that have seen centuries. We didn't go inside; just spent a slow hour walking the gardens. Lunch was at a small Nepalese restaurant nearby — thali, naan, dal, the kind of plate that resets you mid-sightseeing.

Then came Don Quijote. If you've never set foot inside, imagine five floors of everything: snacks, skincare, costumes, electronics, suitcases, and weirdly, more snacks. We walked in for KitKats and walked out with two bags. The KitKat flavour wall alone deserves its own travel guide — matcha, sake, strawberry cheesecake, wasabi. We restrained ourselves to about eight flavours. A small victory.

By evening we needed a quieter pause and found Mercy Vegan Cafe — small, warm, exactly the kind of place that resets you. Then, finally, Dotonbori. No description does it justice. The Glico man, the giant crab sign, the boats slipping under bridges while neon reflects off black water. We just wandered for hours, ate street food, and watched the city turn into one giant lightshow.

Tokyo is order. Kyoto is calm. Osaka is the friend who pulls you into the night, hands you street food, and laughs the loudest. You don't visit Osaka — you hang out with it.

Must-Do in Osaka

  • Rinku Premium Outlet: Best shoe shopping in Japan, hands down.
  • Osaka Castle Grounds: Walk the moats even if you skip the interior.
  • Rikuro's Cheesecake: Watch the flip, eat it warm.
  • Don Quijote: KitKat hunting + everything else.
  • Dotonbori at Night: Glico man, river boats, street food.
  • Mercy Vegan Cafe: Plant-based escape in central Osaka.
  • Daimaru Department Store: For the food hall alone.
  • Shinsaibashi Arcade: Covered shopping street — perfect for rainy days.
